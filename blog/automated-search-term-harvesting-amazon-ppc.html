1. What is Search Term Harvesting in Amazon PPC?

In Amazon advertising, there is a fundamental difference between a Keyword Target and a Customer Search Term:

  • Keyword Target: What you bid on inside Campaign Manager (e.g., broad match running shoes).
  • Customer Search Term: The exact query the shopper typed into Amazon's search bar (e.g., wide toe box lightweight running shoes for men size 10).

Discovery match types (Auto and Broad) cast a wide net across thousands of unpredictable customer queries. Search Term Harvesting is the systematic engineering process of identifying the small percentage of customer search terms that consistently generate profitable orders and "graduating" them into high-control Exact Match campaigns.

🎯 THE CORE OBJECTIVE OF HARVESTING

Move proven, profitable volume away from loose, unpredictable algorithm matching into dedicated ad groups where you possess 100% control over the exact bid, placement multiplier, and ad budget.

2. The Cannibalization Trap: Why 85% of Sellers Fail at Harvesting

Most sellers execute harvesting completely wrong. Their workflow looks like this:

  1. They see a search term that made 3 sales in an Auto campaign.
  2. They add that search term as an Exact match keyword in a Manual campaign.
  3. They leave the Auto campaign running untouched.

Here is the silent catastrophe that happens inside Amazon's ad auction when you do this:

⚠️ THE INTERNAL AUCTION OVERLAP TRAP

Because the Auto campaign still matches that query, your Auto campaign and your Exact match campaign now compete against each other in the same auction. Amazon's ad server splits click data across two campaigns, starves your Exact match keyword of budget, and inflates your Cost Per Click (CPC).

Without negative query fencing, harvesting does not create efficiency—it creates internal chaos and inflated ad spend.

3. The 4-Tier Keyword Isolation Architecture

To build a scalable, automated harvesting engine, your account must follow a clean 4-tier campaign architecture:

Tier 1: Auto Discovery Campaigns
Low-bid exploration using Loose Match, Close Match, Substitutes, and Complements. Discovers net-new customer search intent.
Tier 2: Broad & Phrase Research Campaigns
Controlled expansion using 2-to-3 word root phrases. Captures long-tail customer variations around verified semantic roots.
Tier 3: Exact Match Scale Vault
Single Keyword Ad Groups (SKAGs) or tightly grouped Exact targets. High bids, dedicated budget, and aggressive Top-of-Search placement multipliers.
Tier 4: Negative Exact Fencing Layer
Every harvested Exact keyword is immediately added as a Negative Exact in Tier 1 and Tier 2, forcing 100% of future traffic directly into Tier 3.

4. The 3-Order Qualification Gate: What to Harvest and What to Ignore

A single order is not a pattern—it is statistical noise. If you promote every search term that gets 1 order, your account will quickly become cluttered with hundreds of low-intent Exact keywords that never convert again.

Search Term Performance Action Required Strategic Rationale
≥ 3 Orders & ACoS ≤ Target Promote to Exact + Negative Fence Proven converter. Move to Exact vault with aggressive Top-of-Search placement.
1 – 2 Orders & ACoS ≤ Target Leave in Discovery (Observe) Promising, but lacks sufficient statistical confidence. Let it gather 1 more order.
≥ 3 Orders & ACoS > Target Promote to Exact at Lower Bid High intent, but discovery CPC was too high. Moving to Exact allows you to control the bid.
≥ 10 Clicks & 0 Orders Negative Exact Immediately Dead spend. Eliminate wasted budget to protect overall campaign profitability.

5. Negative Exact Query Fencing: The Crucial Execution Step

Negative fencing is what transforms ad hoc keyword hunting into a disciplined, institutional PPC system.

Every time you harvest a search term into your Exact match campaign, you must simultaneously execute this fencing protocol:

⚡ THE FENCING PROTOCOL
  1. Add the qualified search term into your Exact Match Campaign as an Exact keyword.
  2. Navigate to the originating Auto Campaign > Negative Targeting > Add as Negative Exact.
  3. Navigate to the originating Broad/Phrase Campaign > Negative Targeting > Add as Negative Exact.

Why Negative Exact instead of Negative Phrase?
Never use negative phrase during keyword harvesting! If you harvest "organic garlic powder" and negative phrase match it in your discovery campaign, you will inadvertently block "organic garlic powder bulk 5lb" and "best organic garlic powder for seasoning", choking off future discoveries. Always use Negative Exact for query fencing.

6. Mathematical Bid Calibration for Harvested Keywords

When moving a keyword into an Exact campaign, how do you decide what bid to set?

Never copy the discovery CPC or guess arbitrary bids. Use the engineering formula based on actual historical conversion performance:

The Exact Match Starting Bid Formula

Harvested Base Bid = Target ACoS × Average Order Value (AOV) × Search Term CVR

Scenario: Your target ACoS is 25% (0.25), your product sells for $40.00, and the harvested search term generated 4 orders from 20 clicks in the discovery report (CVR = 20% or 0.20):
Base Bid = 0.25 × $40.00 × 0.20 = $2.00.
Starting at $2.00 guarantees that at its proven conversion rate, the keyword will hit your exact 25% target ACoS immediately.

7. Step-by-Step Search Term Harvesting SOP

Follow this step-by-step operating procedure during your weekly or bi-weekly optimization cycle:

Step 1: Export Search Term Report
Download a 30-day Sponsored Products Search Term Report from Amazon Ad Console. Exclude the last 48 hours to account for attribution lag.
Step 2: Filter for Winners (Harvest List)
Filter for: Orders ≥ 3 AND ACoS ≤ Target ACoS. Sort by 7-Day Total Sales descending.
Step 3: Filter for Bleeders (Negative List)
Filter for: Clicks ≥ 10 AND Orders = 0. These are your immediate Negative Exact additions.
Step 4: Upload Exact Matches & Apply Fencing
Bulk-upload the Harvest List into your Exact match campaigns with calculated bids. Simultaneously bulk-upload them as Negative Exact into source Auto/Broad campaigns.

10. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What happens if a harvested Exact keyword stops converting? +

If a harvested Exact match keyword experiences performance decay, lower its bid by 15% to 20% in increments. Do not immediately delete the negative exact fence in discovery campaigns, as this causes the query to leak back into loose matching at unpredictable CPCs.

Can I harvest ASINs (product targets) using this same SOP? +

Yes! When customer search terms show competitor ASINs (e.g., b08xyz1234) generating profitable orders, graduate them into a dedicated Product Attribute Targeting (PAT) campaign and add the ASIN as a Negative Product Target in your Auto campaign.

Should I put each harvested keyword in its own campaign? +

For your top 10 to 20 "hero" search terms that generate 60%+ of account revenue, dedicated Single Keyword Campaigns (SKCs) allow you to allocate specific daily budgets and Top-of-Search placement multipliers. For lower-volume long-tail terms, grouping 5 to 10 closely related exact keywords into an ad group is acceptable.
