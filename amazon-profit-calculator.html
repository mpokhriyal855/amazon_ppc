How to Use the Amazon Profit Calculator

Start with the numbers you can verify directly from Seller Central and your own cost records. More accurate inputs produce more useful profitability estimates.

1. Enter Your Product Economics

Input your selling price and true landed product cost (including COGS, packaging, and inbound shipping to warehouse).

2. Add Amazon & Fulfilment Costs

Select your fulfilment method (FBA, Easy Ship, or Self Ship) and verify referral and closing fees against Seller Central slabs.

3. Add Advertising & Return Assumptions

Use recent product-level ACoS or TACoS percentages rather than account-wide averages to get a realistic picture.

4. Review Profit, Break-Even & Sensitivity

Use the resulting break-even ACoS and CPC limits as decision-support boundaries for your Amazon campaigns.

Amazon Profit Calculator Formulas

Estimated Net Profit = Selling Price − (Product Cost + Amazon Fees + Fulfilment + Advertising + Return Loss)
Net Profit Margin = (Estimated Net Profit ÷ Selling Price) × 100
Break-Even ACoS = (Pre-Ad Contribution Margin ÷ Selling Price) × 100
Maximum (Break-Even) CPC = Selling Price × Conversion Rate × Break-Even ACoS
FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Amazon Profit Calculator FAQs

How is Amazon profit calculated? +
Amazon profit is calculated by subtracting product landed cost, Amazon referral fees, closing fees, fulfilment/shipping costs, advertising spend, and return loss from your selling price.
Which Amazon fees should I include? +
Include Referral Fee (percentage based on category), Closing Fee (based on price slab), FBA Fulfilment / Weight Handling fee (or Easy Ship fee), and storage charges.
What is break-even ACoS? +
Break-even ACoS is the exact ACoS percentage equal to your pre-ad contribution margin. At break-even ACoS, advertising spend consumes all profit, resulting in zero net profit and zero loss.
How is maximum CPC calculated? +
Maximum (Break-Even) CPC is calculated by multiplying Revenue per Click (Selling Price × Conversion Rate) by Break-Even ACoS. Target CPC uses your target ACoS instead.
How do returns affect Amazon profit? +
Returns add unrecoverable shipping costs, customer refund fees, and inventory write-off losses. Even a 3–5% return rate can reduce unit profit by ₹15–₹30.
