QUICK SUMMARY

TACoS measures total ad spend relative to total Amazon revenue (organic + paid combined). While ACoS evaluates immediate ad ROI, TACoS measures whether your advertising is building sustainable organic keyword ranking or creating artificial ad dependency.

TACoS = Total Ad Spend ÷ Total Revenue × 100

KNOWLEDGE BASE

Frequently Asked Questions About Amazon TACoS

What is Amazon TACoS and why is it more important than ACoS?
TACoS (Total Advertising Cost of Sales) measures ad spend against total store revenue (organic + paid). While ACoS only measures direct ad ROI, TACoS measures your overall brand profitability and whether PPC spend is successfully building organic keyword rank.
What is a good TACoS benchmark for Amazon sellers in India?
For mature Amazon products, a healthy TACoS is between 8% and 15%. During new product launches, TACoS of 20% to 35% is acceptable to index for main keywords. Category leaders often maintain TACoS under 6%.
How do I lower my TACoS without dropping organic rankings?
Lower TACoS by shifting budget into high-intent converting keywords, improving listing main images to boost CTR, adding negative exact keywords for non-converting search terms, and driving external traffic (Google/Meta ads) to boost Amazon organic rank.
Why is my TACoS rising even though my ACoS is improving?
This indicates organic sales erosion. While your ad campaigns are running efficiently, your organic search position is slipping—meaning total revenue is shrinking faster than ad spend.
What is the Organic Sales Multiplier?
The Organic Multiplier measures how many rupees of total revenue are generated for every rupee of ad-attributed sales (`Total Sales ÷ Ad Sales`). A 2.5x multiplier means ₹1 of paid sales creates ₹1.50 of organic sales.
