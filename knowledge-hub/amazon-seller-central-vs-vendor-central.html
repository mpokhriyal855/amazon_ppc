SECTION 01 · EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & CORE DISTINCTION

1. Quick Answer: What Is the Difference Between Seller Central and Vendor Central?

If an Indian business owner or enterprise executive asks for the core difference in one sentence: On Amazon Seller Central, you sell directly to shoppers as a third-party retailer (3P); on Amazon Vendor Central, you sell in bulk directly to Amazon as a wholesale supplier (1P), and Amazon resells those products to consumers.

While both portals ultimately display products on the same Amazon.in marketplace, the underlying commercial, operational, legal, and financial mechanisms are entirely distinct:

SELLER CENTRAL (3P) OPEN REGISTRATION

Direct-to-Consumer Marketplace

  • Relationship: Business-to-Consumer (B2C). You sell directly to the customer.
  • Product Page Byline: "Sold by [Your Business] and Fulfilled by Amazon / Delivered by Amazon".
  • Pricing Autonomy: You retain 100% control over consumer retail prices.
  • Inventory Title: You own the inventory until the customer transaction completes.
  • Disbursements: Rolling 7 to 14-day settlement cycle after delivery.
VENDOR CENTRAL (1P) INVITATION ONLY

First-Party Wholesale Supplier

  • Relationship: Business-to-Business (B2B). You sell wholesale to Amazon.
  • Product Page Byline: "Ships from and sold by Amazon.in".
  • Pricing Autonomy: Amazon sets the final consumer retail price at its sole discretion.
  • Inventory Title: Amazon owns the inventory once accepted at their fulfillment centers.
  • Disbursements: Invoiced payment terms: Net 30, Net 60, or Net 90 days.
Critical Distinction: FBA Is NOT Vendor Central

A widespread misconception among new e-commerce founders is assuming that using Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA) converts their business into an Amazon Vendor. This is completely false. Under FBA, you remain a third-party seller on Seller Central; you continue to own the inventory, set your own selling price, and pay Amazon fees for warehousing and delivery. In contrast, under Vendor Central, Amazon literally issues purchase orders to buy your stock in bulk at wholesale rates.

What You Will Master in This Guide

Whether you are an established brand facing an official Vendor invitation or an ambitious manufacturer weighing where to deploy your capital, this operational guide breaks down:

  • How Seller Central and Vendor Central operate behind the login screen.
  • The reality of Vendor Central's invite-only gatekeeping on the official Indian portal (vendorcentral.in).
  • Unit economic models comparing 3P marketplace margins against 1P wholesale purchase order pricing.
  • Cash flow realities: 14-day rolling deposits versus Net 60/90 invoice terms and chargeback deductions.
  • Advertising access differences across Amazon Ads and retail media products.
  • How to execute a strategic Hybrid Model to maximize marketplace dominance without channel conflict.
SECTION 02 · THIRD-PARTY MARKETPLACE ECOSYSTEM

2. What Is Amazon Seller Central?

Amazon Seller Central (sellercentral.amazon.in) is Amazon's self-serve merchant portal that enables any registered business—from home entrepreneurs and direct-to-consumer brands to regional manufacturers—to list and sell products directly to millions of shoppers.

In industry terminology, this is known as the Third-Party (3P) selling model. When a customer purchases your product on Amazon, the commercial sales contract is executed between you (the seller) and the consumer. Amazon acts as the digital marketplace operator, providing the search engine, payment gateway, fraud detection, and customer traffic in exchange for performance-based commissions.

Core Operational Decks Managed in Seller Central

1. Catalog & Listing Control

Sellers create listings, upload high-resolution images, write benefit-driven bullet points, and build A+ Content and Brand Story modules.

2. Retail Pricing Autonomy

You set, test, and alter your selling price in seconds. You can launch temporary coupons, percentage discounts, or run Prime Exclusive Deals at your sole discretion.

3. Inventory & Stock Levels

You control production runs, decide how many units to ship, monitor sell-through velocity, and decide when to replenish to prevent stockouts.

4. Programmatic Advertising

Full self-serve access to Amazon Campaign Manager for Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display, and video advertising.

Fulfilment Flexibility Within Seller Central

One of Seller Central's greatest assets is logistics choice. As detailed in our companion architectural manual, What Is Amazon Seller Central and How Does It Work?, 3P sellers can fulfill orders using three distinct pathways:

  • Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA): You send inventory to Amazon's regional fulfillment centers (FCs). Amazon handles pick, pack, 1-to-2 day delivery under the Prime badge, and customer returns. You retain product ownership throughout.
  • Amazon Easy Ship: You store goods at your own facility or warehouse. When orders arrive, you pack them, and an Amazon Logistics courier collects the package from your doorstep.
  • Self Ship (Merchant Fulfilled): You store, pack, and ship using approved third-party couriers (such as BlueDart, Delhivery, or DTDC), maintaining manual tracking updates.

SECTION 03 · FIRST-PARTY WHOLESALE SUPPLY

3. What Is Amazon Vendor Central?

Amazon Vendor Central (vendorcentral.in) is Amazon's dedicated B2B enterprise portal designed exclusively for manufacturers, brand owners, and major distributors who act as first-party (1P) wholesale suppliers to Amazon.

Under the 1P model, your commercial transaction is not with the individual consumer who buys the product on Amazon.in. Your buyer is Amazon itself.

How the Vendor Central Workflow Operates

  1. Wholesale Agreement & Terms: You negotiate wholesale cost prices, co-op marketing allowances, freight terms, and return allowances with an Amazon Vendor Manager.
  2. Automated Purchase Orders (POs): Amazon's replenishment algorithms forecast consumer demand and automatically transmit bulk Purchase Orders (weekly or bi-weekly) through Electronic Data Interchange (EDI) or the Vendor Central portal.
  3. Shipment Preparation & Routing: You confirm available quantities, generate Advanced Shipping Notices (ASNs), affix GS1-128 pallet labels, and dispatch bulk freight to specified Amazon fulfillment centers.
  4. Amazon Sells as the Retailer: Once inventory is scanned at the FC, title transfers to Amazon. The product listing prominently states: "Ships from and sold by Amazon.in".
  5. Algorithmic Retail Pricing: Amazon determines the consumer-facing price. If a competitor discounts the product on Flipkart, Blinkit, or offline retail, Amazon's pricing scrapers will automatically lower the retail price on Amazon.in to match.
  6. Invoicing & Payment: You submit wholesale invoices via Vendor Central. Amazon pays you according to your contractual credit terms (e.g., Net 60 days).
Is Vendor Central Open for Public Registration?

No. Vendor Central is strictly invitation-only. There is no public registration form, sign-up button, or fee you can pay to open an account. Amazon's internal category merchant teams identify high-performing brands—frequently observing massive organic search volume, existing bestselling 3P Seller Central metrics, or prominent national retail market share—and reach out directly to corporate leadership. If any agency or consultant promises they can "register you for Vendor Central" for a fee, exercise extreme caution.

Official portal documentation and API endpoints for verified vendors can be reviewed directly in Amazon's Official Vendor Central Documentation.

SECTION 05 · COMMERCIAL ECONOMICS & UNIT MARGINS

5. Seller Central vs Vendor Central: Pricing and Profitability

The financial architecture of Seller Central and Vendor Central represents fundamentally different commercial business models. A business owner cannot simply compare top-line turnover; you must model net cash retained per unit sold.

How Pricing Operates Across Both Models

Seller Central (3P) Pricing Mechanics

As a 3P seller, you set the consumer-facing retail price on Amazon.in. Your revenue is the final selling price paid by the customer, from which Amazon deducts:

  • Category Referral Fee: 2% to 22% commission (Amazon India Fees).
  • Closing Fee: Slab-based fee (₹5 to ₹61+ per order).
  • Shipping & Fulfilment: FBA or Easy Ship weight handling fees.
  • 18% GST: Applied to all Amazon marketplace services.

Vendor Central (1P) Pricing Mechanics

As a 1P vendor, your revenue is the negotiated wholesale cost price at which you sell to Amazon. You do not collect customer payments. However, vendor agreements typically incorporate contractual deductions:

  • Co-Op Marketing Allowance: 4% to 12% deducted for Amazon promotional placement.
  • Freight & Damage Allowance: 2% to 5% deducted for handling and customer returns.
  • Retail Pricing Exposure: Amazon sets consumer prices. If Amazon discounts retail prices to match competitors, they may request cost concessions on future purchase orders.

Worked Unit Economics: Comparing a ₹1,000 Retail Product

Consider an Indian consumer brand producing a Premium Stainless Steel Insulated Flask with a landed manufacturing cost of ₹320. Here is the realistic financial comparison between selling directly on Seller Central at ₹1,000 versus selling wholesale to Amazon via Vendor Central at ₹550 (45% wholesale discount):

SECTION 07 · CASH LIQUIDITY & SETTLEMENT CYCLES

7. Seller Central vs Vendor Central: Payments and Cash Flow

Cash flow management is often the deciding factor when an Indian business evaluates whether to stay on Seller Central or accept a Vendor Central invitation:

Seller Central: 7 to 14-Day Rolling Deposits

Amazon India disburses sales proceeds on a rolling 7-day to 14-day schedule following customer delivery. While Amazon maintains an Account Level Reserve to protect against customer returns, net funds are wired directly into your current account weekly or bi-weekly. This rapid cash conversion cycle enables fast inventory replenishment.

Vendor Central: Net 30, 60, or 90 Days

Amazon operates like a corporate enterprise retailer, negotiating traditional B2B credit terms. Payment terms are typically Net 60 days or Net 90 days from invoice approval. Some agreements include prompt-payment discounts (e.g., 2% discount if Amazon remits within 30 days). A supplier must have sufficient working capital to float manufacturing costs for 60 to 90 days.

The Hidden Friction of Vendor Central Chargebacks

In Vendor Central, Amazon enforces ruthless automated operational compliance. If your warehouse makes minor routing or labeling errors, Amazon’s systems automatically deduct financial penalties known as Chargebacks from your invoice payouts:

  • ASN Non-Compliance: Advanced Shipping Notices transmitted late or containing mismatched carton counts.
  • Barcode Scannability Issues: Missing or unscannable GS1-128 shipping container labels.
  • Late Delivery / Window Mismatch: Trucks arriving at Amazon FCs outside the scheduled freight appointment window.
  • Prep & Bagging Violations: Failure to bag liquids or apply polybag suffocation warnings as required.

Unprepared suppliers frequently discover that chargebacks and co-op deductions quietly erase 3% to 8% of their expected wholesale turnover.

SECTION 09 · THIRD-PARTY STRENGTHS

9. 10 Proven Advantages of Amazon Seller Central

For over 85% of businesses selling on Amazon India—ranging from bootstrapped direct-to-consumer (D2C) brands to agile regional retailers—Seller Central offers superior operational control and strategic autonomy:

1. Immediate & Open Access

No waiting for a corporate invitation. Any registered business entity with GST and PAN can open an account on sell.amazon.in within 48 hours.

2. 100% Retail Pricing Control

You decide retail pricing, minimum advertised price (MAP), and discount schedules. Amazon cannot arbitrarily slash your retail prices to match competitors.

3. Higher Net Rupee Margins

By capturing consumer retail revenue directly rather than selling at deep wholesale discounts (40%–55%), your net cash profit per unit sold is typically 50% to 100% higher.

4. Fast 7 to 14-Day Settlement

Cash from sales lands in your bank account weekly or bi-weekly, accelerating your working capital cycle and funding rapid inventory reorders.

5. Complete Inventory Autonomy

You decide how many units to manufacture and ship. You never have to wait for an Amazon automated replenishment algorithm to send a purchase order.

6. Triple Fulfilment Flexibility

Toggle seamlessly between Amazon FBA for fast Prime delivery, Easy Ship for doorstep pickup, or Self Ship for customized bulky goods.

7. Agile Catalog & A+ Updates

Change bullet points, test main product images, update A+ Content, or launch variations instantly without waiting for vendor ticket approvals.

8. Rich Consumer Analytics

Full access to Amazon Brand Analytics, customer search term frequency, search query performance, repeat purchase rates, and market basket analysis.

9. Zero Vendor Chargeback Penalties

You do not suffer automated EDI, ASN, or pallet height financial deductions from your payouts, which routinely burden 1P wholesale vendors.

10. Total Omnichannel Harmony

Because you control Amazon pricing, you avoid predatory price matching wars that cause friction with your offline distributors and retail stores.

SECTION 10 · OPERATIONAL BURDENS OF 3P

10. Disadvantages & Operational Challenges of Amazon Seller Central

While Seller Central provides unmatched autonomy, it demands rigorous operational competence. As a 3P seller, the full operational burden of retail e-commerce rests on your shoulders:

  • Inventory & Dead-Stock Liability: You bear 100% of physical inventory risk. If products do not sell, Amazon bills you monthly storage fees and aged inventory surcharges until you pay to remove or liquidate them.
  • Direct Customer Return Management: You absorb customer return shipping costs and RTO freight on cash-on-delivery orders. You must inspect damaged returns and repackage salvageable stock.
  • Buy Box Competition: If authorized or unauthorized resellers list on your ASIN, you must compete algorithmically for the Buy Box based on price, Prime delivery speed, and seller metrics.
  • Strict Account Health Metrics: Maintaining low Order Defect Rate (ODR < 1%), Late Dispatch Rate (< 4%), and Pre-fulfillment Cancel Rate requires continuous operational discipline to avoid account suspension.
  • Self-Funded Marketing: Traffic generation is entirely your responsibility. If you do not actively architect and manage Amazon PPC advertising, your listings will remain invisible on search results pages.
SECTION 11 · FIRST-PARTY ENTERPRISE BENEFITS

11. Advantages of Amazon Vendor Central (When Qualified)

For large-scale FMCG corporations, established consumer electronics conglomerates, and enterprise manufacturing plants, Vendor Central offers distinct B2B efficiencies:

1. Hands-Off B2B Wholesale Scale

Move thousands of units in a single bulk Purchase Order. You dispatch truckloads directly to Amazon FCs without handling individual consumer retail parcels.

2. Supreme Consumer Trust

Products carry the prestigious badge: "Ships from and sold by Amazon.in". Millions of shoppers perceive items sold directly by Amazon as authentic and premium.

3. Buy Box Domination

Amazon’s retail algorithms prioritize Amazon’s own 1P retail offers. Third-party marketplace sellers find it virtually impossible to win the Buy Box against Amazon retail.

4. Zero Consumer Customer Service

Amazon manages 100% of customer support queries, delivery tracking calls, refund processing, and consumer disputes under its retail banner.

5. Simplified Logistics Operations

Manufacturers can utilize their existing pallet-based warehouse shipping docks, eliminating the need to build an in-house e-commerce pick-pack division.

6. Enterprise Retail Media Access

Large vendors frequently gain access to tailored co-marketing initiatives, seasonal retail events (Prime Day / Great Indian Festival promotions), and dedicated Vendor Managers.

SECTION 12 · RISKS & FRICTIONS OF 1P

12. Disadvantages & Strategic Risks of Amazon Vendor Central

Many brands that eagerly accepted a Vendor Central invitation later regret the transition due to loss of commercial control. Consider these serious operational risks:

  • Total Loss of Consumer Pricing Control: Amazon’s pricing bots scour the internet 24/7. If a third-party seller discounts your item on Flipkart, JioMart, or Blinkit, Amazon automatically drops the retail price on Amazon.in. This creates severe channel conflict with your physical distributor network who accuse your brand of predatory online pricing.
  • Extended Cash Flow Floats: Waiting Net 60 or Net 90 days for invoice settlement forces suppliers to tie up enormous working capital in raw materials and manufacturing lines.
  • Punitive Automated Chargebacks: Mislabeled pallets, packaging tape deviations, or missed dock delivery appointments trigger non-negotiable chargeback penalties that can wipe out 5% or more of invoice value.
  • Unpredictable Purchase Orders: Amazon’s algorithms order based on proprietary forecasts. Amazon may order 2,000 units one week, causing your factory to scramble, and zero units the following month, leaving your warehouse overflowing with un-ordered stock.
  • Margin Squeeze During Annual Negotiations: Amazon Vendor Managers routinely demand higher co-op percentages, damage allowances, and price concessions during annual contract renegotiations. If you decline, Amazon may mark your ASINs as CRaP (Can't Realize a Profit) and halt all purchase orders.
SECTION 13 · STRATEGIC COMMERCIAL FIT

13. Which Model Is Better for Manufacturers, Wholesalers, Distributors, and Brand Owners?

The optimal Amazon selling model depends on your commercial infrastructure, capital reserves, manufacturing capacity, and brand strategy:

A. Direct Manufacturers (Handicrafts, Textiles, Metalware, Auto Components)

Recommendation: Seller Central (or Hybrid)
Indian manufacturing clusters—such as brass and metalcraft factories in Moradabad or textile mills in Surat—traditionally operated as pure B2B suppliers. While Vendor Central aligns with pallet-based factory dispatch, selling on Seller Central allows manufacturers to bypass middlemen and capture full consumer retail margins (30% to 50% net gross profit). By partnering with specialist agencies for PPC management, manufacturers can build high-equity direct-to-consumer brands without creating heavy internal marketing divisions.

B. Wholesalers & Bulk Stockists

Recommendation: Seller Central
Wholesalers who do not own proprietary brand trademarks are rarely invited to Vendor Central because Amazon prefers sourcing directly from primary brand manufacturers. Wholesalers thrive on Seller Central by operating agile catalog arbitrage, winning Buy Boxes through competitive multi-pack offers, and dispatching via Amazon Easy Ship.

C. Authorized Multi-Brand Distributors

Recommendation: Seller Central
Distributors with exclusive regional distribution rights should utilize Seller Central to enforce Minimum Advertised Price (MAP) consistency. Vendor Central poses a severe risk to distributors: if Amazon’s algorithmic pricing drops retail prices, the distributor faces severe conflict with brick-and-mortar retail accounts.

D. Private-Label Brands & D2C Startups

Recommendation: 100% Seller Central
D2C companies live and die by customer data, review velocity, dynamic pricing tests, and rapid product iteration. Surrendering pricing and catalog control to Vendor Central destroys the agility of a modern D2C startup. Seller Central with FBA and Brand Registry is the undisputed engine for modern brand creation.

E. Tier-1 FMCG & Enterprise Consumer Brands

Recommendation: Hybrid Model (1P + 3P)
Enterprise conglomerates (e.g., major electronics, packaged foods, personal care) typically run high-volume core SKUs through Vendor Central to satisfy automated national retail demand while maintaining an active Seller Central account to pilot new product launches, protect secondary variations, and liquidate overstock.

SECTION 14 · ADVANCED ENTERPRISE STRATEGY

14. Can You Use Both Seller Central and Vendor Central? (The Hybrid Strategy)

Yes, operating a Hybrid Selling Model (simultaneously managing both 1P Vendor Central and 3P Seller Central) has emerged as the premier strategy for sophisticated marketplace brands in India.

How to Architect an ASIN-Segmented Hybrid Strategy

The fatal error in a hybrid model is listing the exact same product on both portals without segmentation, which causes internal pricing cannibalization and algorithmic Buy Box chaos. Instead, top brands segment their product portfolio:

Product Category / SKU Type Recommended Channel Strategic Business Justification
Top-Selling Core Hero SKUs (High Velocity) Vendor Central (1P) Move truckload bulk volume, guarantee Prime availability, and leverage Amazon retail trust.
New Product Innovations & Launches Seller Central (3P) Amazon algorithms will not issue POs for unproven items. Launch on 3P to build sales history and reviews.
High-Margin Niche Variations (Colors / Sizes) Seller Central (3P) Avoid 1P CRaP (Can't Realize a Profit) status. Retain direct pricing control to preserve healthy margins.
Seasonal Bundles & Multi-Packs Seller Central (3P) Create unique virtual or physical bundles (Festival gift packs) without formal B2B vendor onboarding.
Out-of-Stock Fallback (Emergency Backup) Seller Central (3P) If Amazon FCs run out of 1P inventory and fail to issue a PO in time, switch on your 3P FBA offer to prevent lost BSR rank.
SECTION 15 · MIGRATION & ACCOUNT TRANSITIONS

15. Can You Switch From Seller Central to Vendor Central (or Vice Versa)?

Transitioning between marketplace models is not an automatic toggle switch; it represents a major commercial pivot requiring contractual and logistical restructuring:

Transitioning from 3P (Seller) to 1P (Vendor)

You cannot simply click a button in Seller Central to convert to Vendor Central. An Amazon Vendor Manager must formally reach out, evaluate your catalog demand, and issue a Vendor Agreement. If you decide to accept:

  • Do not abruptly close or delete your Seller Central account. Keep it active as an emergency fallback channel.
  • Carefully model the proposed wholesale cost prices against co-op marketing deductions, return allowances, and Net 60/90 payment terms.
  • Ensure your warehouse management system (WMS) supports Electronic Data Interchange (EDI) and automated ASN barcode generation to avoid chargebacks.

Transitioning from 1P (Vendor) Back to 3P (Seller)

Over the past three years, dozens of prominent brands have voluntarily exited Vendor Central to reclaim retail pricing control on Seller Central. However, transitioning back from 1P to 3P involves distinct hurdles:

  • Residual Amazon Inventory: Amazon will continue selling down whatever remaining inventory it owns in its FCs. Until that stock clears, Amazon will dominate the Buy Box.
  • Historic Price Matching Memory: If Amazon heavily discounted your ASIN while acting as the 1P retailer, Amazon’s automated Fair Pricing Algorithm may temporarily flag your new 3P retail price as "too high" if you attempt to restore original MRP.

16. How to Evaluate Which Amazon Model Is Right for Your Business

To select the optimal selling path without costly trial and error, evaluate your organization against this 10-point strategic diagnostic framework:

Strategic Evaluation Question Choose Seller Central (3P) If... Choose Vendor Central (1P) If...

18. Frequently Asked Questions (15 Direct Answers)

Here are straightforward, verified answers to the 15 most common questions business owners ask regarding Seller Central versus Vendor Central:

1. What is the difference between Amazon Seller Central and Vendor Central?

Seller Central is a third-party (3P) retail model where your business sells products directly to Amazon shoppers, setting retail prices and retaining inventory ownership. Vendor Central is a first-party (1P) wholesale model where your business acts as a manufacturer or distributor supplying goods wholesale to Amazon via purchase orders (POs), and Amazon resells those products as the retailer.

2. Which is better, Seller Central or Vendor Central?

Neither platform is universally superior; each serves distinct commercial operating models. Seller Central is ideal for brand owners, D2C companies, and retailers who demand complete control over retail pricing, listing content, customer feedback, and unit profit margins. Vendor Central suits large manufacturers, FMCG brands, and high-volume industrial suppliers equipped to handle bulk B2B purchase orders without managing consumer retail operations.

3. Is Amazon Vendor Central invitation-only?

Yes. Amazon Vendor Central is strictly invitation-only. You cannot register or apply publicly on your own. Amazon's internal category merchant teams initiate invitations based on proprietary market share data, brand search volume, and catalog demand. Be cautious of third-party agencies claiming to sell guaranteed Vendor Central accounts.

4. Can I create a Vendor Central account in India?

You cannot self-register or create a Vendor Central account in India independently. The official Indian portal (vendorcentral.in) is accessible only to suppliers who have received an official onboarding invitation and contract from an Amazon India Vendor Manager.

5. Can a small business use Amazon Seller Central?

Yes, absolutely. Seller Central is universally open to Indian sole proprietorships, partnerships, LLPs, and private limited companies. Anyone with a valid PAN, GST registration, active bank account, and pickup address can register for free on sell.amazon.in and start selling directly.

6. Who owns inventory in Seller Central?

The seller owns 100% of the inventory at all times until it is purchased by the final customer. Even when utilizing Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA), Amazon merely acts as a third-party warehousing and logistics service provider; Amazon never takes legal title to your products.

7. Who owns inventory in Vendor Central?

Amazon owns the inventory once it is delivered and accepted at their fulfillment centers pursuant to a Purchase Order. Amazon takes legal ownership and assumes the physical inventory risk, although vendors remain bound by contractual return and defective inventory terms.

8. Who controls product pricing in Vendor Central?

Amazon has complete algorithmic and commercial control over consumer-facing retail prices in Vendor Central. While you agree on a wholesale supply price with Amazon, Amazon can discount, match competitor prices, or adjust consumer prices without your consent.

9. Who controls pricing in Seller Central?

The seller retains complete control over retail offer pricing, subject only to marketplace Fair Pricing Policies. You can raise, lower, run coupon discounts, or modify product prices in real time directly from your Seller Central dashboard.

10. Is FBA the same as Vendor Central?

No. FBA (Fulfillment by Amazon) is a logistics fulfillment service available within Seller Central. Under FBA, you remain the third-party seller, retain inventory ownership, set retail prices, and pay Amazon a fee to store, pick, pack, and deliver. In Vendor Central, Amazon purchases the inventory wholesale and resells it as the retailer.

11. Can I use Seller Central and Vendor Central at the same time?

Yes, many established brands operate a selective hybrid model. Brands frequently use Vendor Central for core high-volume staple products while retaining Seller Central for new product launches, seasonal variations, direct pricing control, and clearing excess stock.

12. How does Amazon pay Seller Central sellers?

Amazon disburses marketplace sales proceeds on a rolling 7-day to 14-day settlement cycle after customer delivery, directly wiring net funds (after deducting referral, closing, and shipping fees) into the seller's verified Indian bank account.

13. How does Amazon pay Vendor Central suppliers?

Vendors are paid based on negotiated B2B credit terms typically ranging from Net 30, Net 60, to Net 90 days following invoice submission. In some agreements, Amazon offers early payment discounts (such as a 2% discount for payment within 30 days).

14. Can I switch from Seller Central to Vendor Central?

You cannot arbitrarily switch from Seller Central to Vendor Central on your own. You can only transition to Vendor Central if Amazon approaches your brand with an official vendor invitation and you mutually negotiate wholesale terms.

15. Which Amazon model is better for manufacturers and wholesalers?

If a manufacturer has existing B2B production lines and lacks an in-house e-commerce team, Vendor Central provides hands-off wholesale scale. However, many modern manufacturers choose Seller Central (or partner with specialized agencies like PPC Growth Expert) to capture 20% to 35% higher gross margins and maintain direct brand control.
