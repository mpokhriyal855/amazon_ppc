Why Are My Amazon PPC Impressions and Clicks Low?

If your Amazon PPC impressions and clicks are low, your campaigns may not be getting enough visibility in Amazon search results or product placements. Low impressions usually indicate a visibility problem, while low clicks can indicate a relevance, CTR, product appeal, or targeting problem. Before increasing your budget, you should identify where the issue is: Targeting → Bid → Budget → Relevance → Placement → CTR → Listing. At PPC Growth Expert, we analyze these factors to identify why your campaigns aren't receiving enough qualified traffic and what needs to be optimized.

✦ TL;DR — QUICK ANSWER

Your Amazon PPC impressions or clicks may be low because of:

  • Low keyword search volume & low bids
  • Highly competitive keywords & low keyword relevance
  • Limited campaign budget & incorrect targeting

  • Weak placement visibility & low Click-Through Rate (CTR)

The important thing is to determine whether the problem is not enough impressions or impressions that aren't turning into clicks.

What Is the Difference Between Impressions and Clicks?

METRIC #1

Impressions

An impression means your Amazon advertisement was shown to a shopper on search results or product pages.

METRIC #2

Clicks

A click occurs when a shopper clicks your advertisement to view your product detail page.

THE COMPLETE PPC FUNNEL
Impressions → CTR → Clicks → Product Page → Orders → Sales
  • If impressions are low: Investigate visibility (bids, volume, budget, targeting).
  • If impressions are healthy but clicks are low: Investigate CTR and relevance (image, title, price, reviews).
  • If clicks are coming but orders are low: Investigate conversion and listing performance.

1. Are Your Amazon PPC Bids Too Low?

One of the most common reasons for low impressions is an uncompetitive bid. Suppose competitors are aggressively bidding on a keyword and your bid is significantly lower—your campaign may struggle to receive enough impressions in active auctions.

However, increasing bids on every keyword is not the answer. Instead, identify:

  • High-potential keywords
  • High-converting keywords
  • High-volume keywords
  • Keywords with low impression volume
  • Keywords with strong ROAS
  • Keywords where additional visibility could generate sales

Then increase bids selectively rather than making blanket account-wide bid increases.

2. Is Your Keyword Search Volume Too Low?

Not every keyword has enough Amazon search volume. For example, a generic broad keyword like “gaming keyboard” may have significantly more searches than a hyper-specific phrase like “wireless gaming keyboard with RGB mechanical blue switches for office PC”.

If you're targeting extremely specific long-tail keywords, impressions may naturally be limited. This doesn't mean long-tail keywords are bad—they can carry strong purchase intent. The solution is to create a balance between:

High-volume keywords
Medium-volume keywords
Long-tail keywords
Exact-match keywords
Phrase-match keywords
Relevant product targets

3. Are Your Keywords Too Specific?

Highly specific keywords can restrict your potential traffic. For example, targeting “black wireless gaming mouse under ₹1500” may have very few daily searches compared to “wireless gaming mouse”.

A good Amazon PPC strategy often uses multiple keyword layers rather than relying on only one type of keyword:

  • High-Volume Keywords: Useful for reach and broad market visibility.
  • Long-Tail Keywords: Useful for specific purchase intent and higher conversion.
  • High-Converting Search Terms: Useful for scaling proven customer queries.
💡 KEYWORD BALANCE

The goal is to find the right balance between traffic volume and traffic quality across your campaigns.

4. Is Your Keyword Relevant to Your Product?

Amazon needs to determine whether your product is relevant to the shopper's search. If your keyword and product are not closely aligned, your campaign may struggle to receive meaningful impressions.

For example, if you sell a Laptop Cooling Pad but target Laptop Stand, the two products may be related, but they are not the same product. Broad targeting can help discover opportunities, but your account should still maintain strong product relevance to ensure Amazon's algorithm awards top placement impressions.

5. Is Your Campaign Budget Limiting Impressions?

Budget can also affect campaign visibility. Suppose a campaign is generating strong traffic and sales but repeatedly reaches its daily budget early in the day—you may be losing opportunities to show your ads during peak evening hours.

However, increasing the budget of a poor-performing campaign isn't necessarily the solution. First evaluate:

Spend → Sales → Orders → ACOS → ROAS → Budget utilization
  • If a campaign is performing well but budget-limited, increasing its budget makes immediate sense.
  • If a campaign has poor ROAS and low conversion, fix the underlying bid or keyword problem before increasing spend.

6. Are You Targeting Extremely Competitive Keywords?

Some high-level keywords have intense bidding competition (e.g. Laptop, Gaming Mouse, Headphones, Keyboard, Smartwatch). These keywords can have dozens of established advertisers competing for top placements.

If your bid is not competitive enough, impressions can remain low. Instead of competing aggressively for every high-volume keyword, consider building a portfolio of:

High-volume + Medium-volume + Long-tail + Branded + Product targeting

This gives your account more opportunities to generate impressions without depending entirely on expensive high-competition keywords.

7. Is Your Campaign Structure Limiting Performance?

Poor campaign structure can make PPC optimization difficult. For example, if dozens or hundreds of unrelated keywords are placed into the same campaign, it becomes harder to control bids, budgets, match types, search terms, and scaling.

A more structured account allows you to answer:

  • Which keywords need higher bids?
  • Which keywords need lower bids?
  • Which campaigns deserve more budget?
  • Which targets need to be isolated?

Your campaign structure should give you enough granular control to make these decisions effectively.

8. Are Your Match Types Limiting Your Traffic?

Amazon keyword match types influence how your ads can match shopper searches:

Exact Match

Provides high control but can restrict potential traffic volume.

Phrase Match

Provides a balanced mix between control and search discovery.

Broad Match

Provides wider discovery but requires negative keyword management.

If your account relies too heavily on exact-match keywords with low search volume, impressions can be severely limited. A combination of match types helps create a healthier balance between search discovery and cost control.

9. Are Your Amazon PPC Clicks Low Because CTR Is Low?

Suppose your campaign gets 100,000 impressions but only 100 clicks. Your impressions are not the problem—your Click-Through Rate (CTR) is.

CTR FORMULA

CTR = (Clicks ÷ Impressions) × 100

Example: 100 Clicks ÷ 100,000 Impressions = 0.10% CTR

In this situation, investigate: Main image, Product relevance, Price, Reviews, Product title, Brand recognition, Search intent, and Competition. The shopper is seeing your ad but isn't sufficiently motivated to click.

10. Can Your Main Product Image Affect PPC Clicks?

Yes. Your main image is one of the very first elements shoppers see when your product appears in search results.

Imagine two competing products:

  • Product A: Crisp, professional, high-resolution main image showcasing key features.
  • Product B: Dull, cluttered, or unclear main image.

Even if both products receive similar impressions, Product A will consistently generate a higher CTR. If impressions are healthy but clicks are low, auditing your main image is one of the highest-leverage fixes available.

11. Is Your Price Affecting Your Amazon PPC CTR?

Price strongly influences whether shoppers click your ad when comparing search results.

Suppose search results show:

  • Competitor A: ₹999 (4.5★ reviews)
  • Your Product: ₹1,799 (4.2★ reviews)

If your listing thumbnail and title don't communicate a clear reason for the price difference (e.g. premium materials, additional accessories, superior warranty), shoppers will click competitor results instead. PPC visibility cannot compensate for an uncompetitive offer indefinitely.

Should I Increase My Amazon PPC Bid to Get More Impressions?

Not always. Increasing bids can increase your opportunity to receive impressions, but it can also increase advertising costs without guaranteeing sales.

Before increasing a bid, ask:

  • Is this keyword relevant to the product?
  • Does it have sufficient search volume?
  • Does it generate orders historical data?
  • What is its current CPC?
  • What is its ROAS performance?
  • Is additional traffic commercially valuable?
KEYWORD A (Increase Bid)

₹20 bid → Strong conversion → ROAS 6.0x → Deserves more visibility!

KEYWORD B (Do Not Increase)

₹20 bid → 50 clicks → 0 orders → Does NOT deserve higher bid simply due to low impressions.

How Do I Diagnose Low Impressions vs Low Clicks?

Use this diagnostic framework to pinpoint your bottleneck:

Symptom Root Cause Focus Recommended Action
Low Impressions + Low Clicks Visibility Problem Investigate Bids → Search Volume → Targeting → Budget → Competition
High Impressions + Low Clicks CTR & Relevance Problem Investigate CTR → Main Image → Price → Relevance → Competition
High Clicks + Low Orders Conversion & Listing Problem Investigate Search Terms → Listing → Price → Reviews → Conversion Rate
High Orders + Low Impressions Scaling Opportunity Investigate Bids → Campaign Budget → Keyword Coverage → Scaling

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are my Amazon PPC impressions low? +

Low impressions can be caused by low bids, low search volume, highly competitive keywords, limited budgets, poor targeting, or insufficient keyword coverage.

Why are my Amazon PPC clicks low? +

If impressions are healthy but clicks are low, your Click-Through Rate (CTR) may be weak. Investigate the main image, price, relevance, reviews, product appeal, and competition.

Should I increase my Amazon PPC bids to get more impressions? +

Not automatically. Increase bids selectively for relevant keywords with strong commercial potential rather than increasing every bid across the account.

Can low search volume cause low Amazon PPC impressions? +

Yes. Keywords with limited shopper demand naturally have fewer opportunities to generate impressions.

Can my Amazon listing affect PPC clicks? +

Yes. When impressions are high but CTR is low, elements such as the main image, product relevance, price, reviews, and product positioning can influence click behavior.

Should I use broad, phrase, and exact match keywords? +

A combination can be useful because each match type provides a different balance between discovery and control. The right structure depends on your product, search demand, campaign objective, and historical performance.

How can I increase Amazon PPC impressions without increasing my budget? +

Improve keyword coverage, optimize bids, reduce wasted spend, improve targeting, identify high-performing campaigns, and reallocate existing budget toward stronger opportunities.

Can PPC Growth Expert improve my Amazon PPC impressions and clicks? +

Yes. PPC Growth Expert can analyze your campaigns, keywords, search terms, bids, budgets, placements, CTR, and listing-related factors to identify why impressions or clicks are low and create an optimization strategy.
